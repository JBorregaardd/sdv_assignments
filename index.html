Drug offenses fell in San Francisco, but the pattern narrowed into hotspots

Recorded drug offenses in San Francisco changed in two ways over the last two decades. The total number of incidents dropped sharply after the late 2000s, but the pattern also became less evenly spread across the city and more concentrated in a small number of hotspots.

Introduction

A short story, not a full report

This story looks at recorded drug offenses in San Francisco from 2003 to 2025. The first pattern is easy to see: the total number of incidents rises into the late 2000s, then falls strongly throughout the 2010s, and finally turns upward again in recent years. The other pattern is spacial and very interesting to look at. As the total number of incidents fell, the remaining incidents became more concentrated in a smaller set of locations rather than disappearing evenly across the city.

That does not mean the data reflects drug activity directly. These are police-recorded incidents, so the pattern can reflect enforcement, reporting, and where police attention is concentrated, not just where drug activity happens. Still, the data is useful for showing how the recorded geography of drug offenses changed over time.

Visualization 1 · Static chart

The long-run trend sets up the story

The first figure gives the overall time trend. Drug offenses are high in the early and late 2000s, reach a peak around 2009, and then fall sharply through the 2010s. By 2021, the number of recorded incidents is much lower than at the peak. After that, the curve turns upward again, with a noticeable rebound in the most recent years.

On its own, that looks like a simple rise-and-fall story. But the main point of this page is that the decline was not evenly spread across the city. The total number of incidents dropped, but the pattern that remained became more concentrated. So this figure works best as a starting point: it shows that the overall volume changed a lot, but still not how the geography of those incidents changed with it yet.

Visualization 2 · Map

The map shows that the story is not evenly distributed

After the broad time trend, the page shifts to geography. This is the natural second step in a magazine-style story: readers already know that something changed, and now they can see where the pattern is concentrated. A map is good at making spatial clustering intuitive in a way that tables or paragraph descriptions are not.

In your final version, this paragraph should interpret the strongest spatial pattern without overstating it. Focus on what the map reveals visually: which areas stand out, whether the concentration is stable or shifting, and how that adds nuance to the first chart. Avoid turning a hotspot into a moral or policy conclusion unless your analysis truly supports it.

Interpretation note: a dense hotspot on a crime map can reflect many things besides underlying prevalence, including policing strategy and reporting intensity. The design should make the pattern visible, while the text keeps the claim careful.
Visualization 3 · Interactive Plotly chart

The final graphic opens up controlled exploration

The last figure gives the reader a bit more agency. This is a nice way to end the story because the main argument has already been made by the first two visuals. The interactive chart does not need to carry the narrative by itself. Instead, it acts as a structured invitation to inspect neighborhoods, categories, or ranked trends more closely.

That balance is exactly what Week 8 is pushing toward: an author-driven page with limited reader freedom. The text guides what matters, while the interaction rewards curiosity. In the finished version, you can use this paragraph to tell the reader how to engage with the chart and what kind of comparison is worth trying first.

Closing reflection

What the page should leave the reader with

A strong ending does not introduce new evidence. It briefly returns to the main point, ties the three visuals together, and reminds the reader what kind of claim the data can support. In practice, that often means one sentence about the big pattern, one sentence about the geographic nuance, and one sentence about why interpretation should remain cautious.
